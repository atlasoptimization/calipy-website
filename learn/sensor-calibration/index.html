Calibration field guide

Plan and validate
a calibration study.

For teams working with sensors and scientific instruments: a practical way to turn a calibration question into a testable measurement model.

Define what you are trying to learn.

Start with the measurand: the physical quantity the experiment is meant to measure. Then distinguish the instrument reading from that quantity. An offset, a sensitivity change, a settling response, or uncertain reference information can each affect the relationship.

Write a concrete question: “What steady level explains these readings?” is a different task from “How well will this calibration transfer to a new sensor?” The question determines the data and validation you need.

Represent the measurement process.

A generative model describes how a physical quantity, instrument response, and sources of variation produce an observation. CaliPy lets you express that relationship in Python with named scientific dimensions and explicit probability distributions.

quantity → response → observation
unknowns + assumptions + noise

For warm-up, an exponential response may be plausible. For another instrument, a linear calibration curve, geometry, or an environmental dependency may be more appropriate. Start with a small model whose terms have a physical or statistical justification.

Measurement noise describes variation in readings. Uncertainty about a model parameter describes what is not yet known about that quantity. Keeping these separate is central to interpreting an uncertainty statement.

Collect measurements that can distinguish the effects.

  • Cover the relevant conditions. A settling time is difficult to learn from observations taken only after equilibrium.
  • Include reference information. If a reference is uncertain, represent that uncertainty instead of silently treating it as exact.
  • Record repetitions and identities. Sensor, object, session, and repeat axes can support different explanations of variation.
  • Plan validation before fitting. Hold out whole sensors, sessions, or conditions when those are the cases you need to generalize to.

More readings do not automatically resolve confounded parameters. If different combinations produce similar observations, reconsider the experiment, the model, or the available prior information.

Match the inference to the claim.

A point fit can estimate a useful response curve and support a baseline comparison. A posterior represents uncertainty under the chosen likelihood and prior. CaliPy’s development interface exposes both styles through its inference plans.

For posterior sampling, examine convergence, effective sample size, divergences where applicable, and sensitivity to priors. For optimization, examine fit stability, initialization sensitivity, and residual structure. A small training loss alone does not establish either scientific adequacy or reliable uncertainty.

Validate the result where it will be used.

Connect the intended use to the validation design
Intended useUseful evidence
Within one measured rangeHeld-out readings, residual patterns, and a simple baseline.
Another session or instrumentIndependent sessions or instruments kept outside model development.
An uncertainty statementDiagnostics, sensitivity studies, and repeated validation of interval behavior where feasible.

Retain the data provenance, model assumptions, software environment, inference settings, and the limits of the evidence. That makes the analysis easier to review and repeat.
