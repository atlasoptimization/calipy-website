CaliPy is a good starting point when a physical or statistical explanation matters and your team can evaluate a Python-based scientific workflow. It is pre-alpha research software; support, integration, and deployment requirements belong in the project scope.

PROPOSED ENGAGEMENT · SCOPE TO AGREE

One measurement problem.
One evidence-led pilot.

A practical starting scope is one instrument or experiment, one bounded dataset, and one clearly defined modeling question. Together, we would agree on a baseline and acceptance criteria before implementation.

  • Frame: identify the quantities, reference information, data structure, and intended decision.
  • Model: construct a small scientific model with explicit assumptions and an appropriate inference method.
  • Evaluate: compare with the agreed baseline and report diagnostics, sensitivity, and validation limits.
  • Hand over: explain what the model supports, what remains uncertain, and which next experiment or integration step would help.

This is a proposed service format for discussion. Availability, schedule, price, licensing, confidentiality, and acceptance terms are agreed individually in a written scope. No booking or payment is taken on this website.

Leave with something your team can inspect.

A runnable model

Python source, environment information, and a reproducible example using an agreed data-sharing arrangement.

An evidence report

Assumptions, inference settings, a baseline comparison, diagnostics, and the limits of the result.

A practical next step

A handover discussion and recommendations for further measurement, model improvement, or integration.

Hosted operation, certification, ongoing support, broader production integration, and rights to redistribute software are separate scope items. The pilot’s exact deliverables would be confirmed before work starts.

Make the collaboration concrete.

  1. Send a short problem description. Describe the instrument and the question. A data summary is enough for the first exchange.
  2. Agree on a feasible study. Discuss data access, the baseline, success criteria, deliverables, commercial terms, and the limits of the method.
  3. Review the model and evidence together. Keep the scientific assumptions visible, then decide whether to extend or integrate the result.
